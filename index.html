ALEXANDER HAMILTON

Founding Father of the United States

Alexander Hamilton was a prominent American statesman, military officer, and one of the most important figures in the early history of the United States. Born in the Caribbean in 1755 or 1757, he rose to national prominence during the American Revolution and later became a leading architect of the new republic.

Hamilton served as the first Secretary of the Treasury from 1789 to 1795. In that role, he created a plan to stabilize the nation's finances, establish the United States Bank, and strengthen the federal government. He also helped shape the country's economic system by promoting trade, taxation, and public credit.

  • He helped lead the Continental Army during the American Revolution.
  • He argued strongly in favor of a powerful central government.
  • He supported the creation of a national financial system and a national bank.
  • He wrote many of the Federalist Papers, which explained and defended the Constitution.
  • He was a major influence in the early development of the United States as a stable nation.

Hamilton's ideas were deeply influential in shaping American political and economic development. He believed that a strong government and a sound financial system were necessary for national growth, liberty, and stability. His work helped lay the foundation for the republic that would continue to evolve in the years to come.
